01 · Only here

Three things only RoarWriter does.

Why a director writes the next script here, and not in a word processor.

01

The keys of Final Draft.

Enter goes on to what usually comes next: after a scene heading the action, after a name the dialogue, after the dialogue the next name. Tab turns a line into another kind, Option or Alt and a number picks one, in the browser too. The format takes care of itself while you write.

02 · The page

A script that reads like a script.

The page as producers, actors and the crew expect it, and the files the rest of the industry uses.

The PDF

Ready to send.

Courier 12 with the standard margins, the title page as Final Draft lays it out, page numbers from page two, A4 or US Letter. The pages are laid out for real, and you see them as you write: a scene heading never ends a page and a name stays with its lines.

2.3.
The files

Final Draft, Fade In and Fountain.

Open a script from Final Draft, Fade In or Fountain, save it back as any of them. Paste a screenplay as plain text and it turns into elements.

.fdx in · out.fadein in · out.fountain in · out.pdf out
At the side

Scenes and characters.

Every scene numbered, the one you are in lit; one click takes you there. Every character with how many times they speak and in how many scenes, renamed everywhere in one go. And the Notes tab, and the Series tab in an episode.

Updates itself 0.5

Writes offline, too.

The Mac and Windows apps carry the whole program inside them: on a plane or on set with no signal, you go on writing, and the script is sent to your account when the connection is back. New versions download by themselves.

03 · The studio

From the page to the shoot.

Version 0.5: what a production asks of a script, in the same app it is written in.

The AI on the whole script new

The whole script, read.

Coverage with a verdict, continuity (who knows what before they learn it, the prop that comes back), a budget pass that finds what is expensive and a cheaper way to the same story value, a pitch package for a fund or a market, a translated copy in Greek or English. Ask the script anything: the scenes it names take you there.

Revisions and versions new

The way a production works.

Revision colours in the industry order, an asterisk on every changed line, scene numbers locked (a new one after 12 is 12A), the revised pages alone as a PDF. Versions of the script, one a day by itself, compared word by word with now.

Planning the film new

Beats, frames, days.

A beat board (Save the Cat, three acts, Hero’s Journey) that shows where each beat should fall and where it does. A storyboard with a picture for every shot, the AI breaking a scene into shots, a shot list for the crew. A stripboard schedule with Day out of Days.

TV new

A series, with its bible.

The episodes of a show in one place, with its logline, its world, the characters and places of every episode, and what is known since which episode, at the side of every episode you write.

04 · Everything

And all a draft needs.

What came in the latest versions, and the rest, briefly.

01Nine formats

Feature film, TV drama, TV half-hour, multi-camera sitcom, stage play, musical, audio drama, AV two-column and comic. Each with its own page, kinds of line, keys and PDF.

02It knows what you mean

INT. or ΕΣΩΤ. at the start of a line makes a scene heading; CUT TO: makes a transition. A parenthetical opens its own brackets.

03Breakdown

Select a word, right-click it and tag it: cast, props, locations, wardrobe and eight more, lit in colour wherever it appears. By scene or by element, and as a spreadsheet.

04Write together

Share a script by email, to edit or only to read. What the others write comes in within seconds, and changes to different lines never overwrite each other.

05Undo, and nothing to save

Every change keeps itself. ⌘Z and ⇧⌘Z go back and forth through what you did.

06Title page on the page

Title and episode, credit and author, copyright, draft and contact, typed where they go, as Final Draft lays them out.

07Final Draft and Fade In

Bring in a .fdx, a .fadein or a .fountain, title page included, go on writing, and save it back as any of them.

08Zoom

− 100% + in the bar, ⌘+ and ⌘−, or a pinch on the trackpad; on a phone, two fingers on the page.

09Spelling in two languages

The apps check Greek and English as you type, with suggestions on a right click.

10On a phone

The page in letters to write in, the kinds of line over the keyboard, scenes and the AI over the screen, and an icon on the home screen.

11In the browser too

The same app on the web, nothing to install, with your scripts in your account.

12AI only when you ask

Nothing is sent to the AI until you press one of its buttons: the scene you are on, or the whole script when you ask for coverage or a question about it. It writes in the script’s format.

13Bold, italic, underline

Inside a line, kept in the PDF, Final Draft and Fountain.

14Dual dialogue

Two characters speaking at once, side by side, with ⌘D.

15Scene alternates

Keep another way a scene could go, or let the AI write one; compare and use it.

16Runtime and tension

The running time from the words, truer with every table read, and the tension of every scene along the minutes.

17Research desk

Facts for the scene from the web, with their sources, kept as a note on it.

18Write by speaking

Dictate in Greek or English; insert it as it is, or let the AI lay it out as script.

19Writers in the room

See who is in which scene right now, and give scenes to writers.

20Find and replace

⌘F and ⌘⌥F, with case and whole words; Greek accents are ignored, so a word is found however it was typed.

21The report

Pages, minutes, scenes, INT and EXT, day and night, locations, and who speaks how much.

22Your page, your PDF

A white, sepia or midnight page to write on; in the PDF a header and a faint watermark, such as CONFIDENTIAL or the reader’s name.

07 · Questions

Good to know.

Is RoarWriter free?

Yes. No subscription, no ads.

Do I need an account?

Yes, a RoarMe account: the same one as RoarMe, Roardino and RoarList. It is what keeps your scripts safe and the same on every device.

Can I open my Final Draft or Fade In scripts?

Yes: import the .fdx or the .fadein and go on writing; save it back as either whenever you need it there. Fountain works both ways too.

Does it write Greek scripts properly?

Yes. Scene headings with ΕΣΩΤ. and ΕΞΩΤ., names in capitals, suggestions in Greek, spelling in Greek in the apps, and a PDF in the same format as an English script.

What does the AI see?

Only what you ask about: the scene you are on and a little of what comes before it, or the whole script when you choose Whole script (coverage, continuity, a question), and only when you press one of its buttons. Its answer appears beside the page; nothing changes until you press Insert or Replace.

Does it work without internet?

The Mac and Windows apps do: you write as usual and the script is sent to your account when the connection is back. The AI needs a connection.

Does it write stage plays and TV?

Yes. When you start a script you choose its format: feature film, TV drama in acts, TV half-hour, multi-camera sitcom, stage play, musical, audio drama, AV two-column or comic. You can change it later on its title page.

Can two of us write the same script?

Yes. Press Share, type their email (their RoarMe account) and choose Can edit or Can view. Only the owner shares or removes someone; anyone can leave.

A4 or US Letter?

Either, chosen per script on its title page or in Settings; what new scripts start with is set in the Settings of the list of scripts. The margins and the font stay those of the standard screenplay format.
