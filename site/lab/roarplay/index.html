01 · Only here

Three things only RoarPlay does.

Why the player that came with the computer is not enough when the file came from a camera.

01

Plays everything.

What the computer decodes itself plays as it is. Everything else is made playable while it plays, on your own computer, with nothing written to disk: a picture the computer knows in a wrapper it does not (H.264 in MKV, AVI, TS) is only re-wrapped; ProRes, DNxHR, MPEG-2 and the rest are made H.264 on the fly, with the Mac’s own encoder; sound the computer cannot play comes as FLAC, lossless. The Info tab says which.

02 · For the director

Made for watching work.

Dailies, a cut, a mix, an old archive: it opens and it tells you what it is.

Info

What the file is made of.

Wrapper, length, bitrate, timecode; the picture’s codec and profile (ProRes HQ, H.264 High), frame size, frame rate, colour, interlacing; the sound’s codec, sample rate, bit depth and channels; subtitles. And how RoarPlay is playing it.

Sound

Cover, waveform, every track.

A sound file shows its cover, its title and artist, and a waveform you scrub. A film with several sound tracks, the Greek and the English, the mix and the M&E: choose one in Info and it switches where you are.

Queue

A folder in order.

Drop files or whole folders; they line up in name order, with length, size and frame. One ends, the next plays, or stops, or repeats. Cmd+← → jump between them.

Honest

Camera RAW: it tells you.

Blackmagic RAW, REDCODE, ARRIRAW, Canon RAW and N-RAW need their makers’ own tools. RoarPlay does not pretend: it names the file’s format and the app that opens it, so you are not left with a black window.

03 · The player’s tools

Everything a player should have had.

Since 0.2: subtitles, speed, chapters, the picture and the sound in your hands, and a window that gets out of the way.

Subtitles

Inside the file or beside it.

The tracks inside an MKV, MOV or MP4 and any .srt, .ass, .vtt or .sub beside the file (Film.srt, Film.el.srt), or one you pick; three sizes, a delay in half seconds. S switches them.

Speed and chapters

0.25× to 4×, voices kept.

Slow a take down or race through an interview with the voices at their own pitch; [ and ] step the speed, \ brings it back. Chapters as ticks on the bar with their names, Page Up and Page Down between them.

Picture

Fit, fill, 2.39, zoom, turn.

An aspect mask (2.39, 2.00, 1.85, 16:9, 4:3, 1:1, 4:5, 9:16) darkens what falls outside the frame without cutting; zoom to 4×; turn, mirror, upside down; brightness, contrast, saturation. The window takes the picture’s shape when a file opens.

Sound

What you hear, exactly.

The track; stereo, left only, right only, mono or swapped, and with 5.1 the centre alone or the surrounds alone; a delay in 50 ms steps; the output device; a ten-band equalizer with presets.

01The small bezel

B: only the picture inside a thin frame, no bars; a small bar with play, the clock and Always on top comes when the mouse moves. P for picture in picture.

02In and out that clear with a click

The handles sit on the bar; a click on one clears it, the × beside them clears both.

03Info, all of it

Everything the file says: the wrapper and its tags, every stream with its flags and side data, chapters, dates, the whole ffmpeg report. Copy all puts it on the clipboard.

04The queue, kept

The list is there next time, with shuffle, and can be saved as M3U; M3U files open as a list. A file stopped in the middle opens there again.

05Thumbnails on the bar

Move over the bar and see the frame you are about to jump to.

06Open with RoarPlay

One switch in Settings makes RoarPlay the app for video or sound files; a double click in the Finder or the Explorer opens them here.

08 · Questions

Good to know.

Is RoarPlay free?

Yes. No subscription, no ads, no account.

Does it really play ProRes and MXF on Windows?

Yes. What the computer cannot decode is made H.264 on the fly by the ffmpeg that comes with the app, on your computer, as it plays. On a Mac the Mac’s own video engine does it; on Windows the software encoder, which keeps up with 1080p and most 4K on a recent machine.

Is the picture I see the original?

H.264, HEVC, VP9 and AV1 in MP4, MOV, MKV or WebM play as they are. ProRes, DNxHR, MPEG-2 and the other edit codecs are re-encoded for the screen at a high quality, so judge colour and sharpness on the original in your grading tool; a frame saved with Cmd+Shift+S is taken from the original file.

Where does the timecode come from?

From the file: the timecode track of a MOV or MXF, or the timecode in its metadata. A file without one counts from 00:00:00:00. Drop-frame is shown with a semicolon, as the camera wrote it.

Does it play Blackmagic RAW or RED?

Not yet. Camera RAW needs the makers’ own tools; RoarPlay tells you which one opens the file. Transcode it to ProRes with RoarMorph or Resolve to play it here.

Is the loudness reading the real one?

The meters read what you hear, K-weighted and gated as EBU R128 says; in our tests the integrated reading matched ffmpeg’s to a tenth of a LU. The QC report measures the whole file once, with true peak. For a delivery, check against the broadcaster’s own tool too.

Where do the subtitles come from?

From inside the file (MKV, MOV, MP4 tracks) and from any .srt, .ass, .vtt or .sub beside it with the same name, or a file you pick. Bitmap subtitles (DVD, Blu-ray) are named but not shown.

Do I need the internet?

No. Only to download the app and its updates; everything else runs on your computer.
